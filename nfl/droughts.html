NFL droughts: last season and completed seasons without each milestone

Seasons use the NFL season year: a Super Bowl played in February 2026 belongs to the 2025 season. The ongoing season is not counted until its Super Bowl is complete. A result in the latest completed season has a drought of 0.

“Never” counts active seasons in the Super Bowl era, starting in 1966 or the franchise’s first season. Conference titles mean a Super Bowl berth, including the NFL/AFL champions in 1966–69. Earlier league championships are not Super Bowl wins. Division titles include former divisions. Franchise moves and name changes stay with the same team; the Browns’ 1996–98 suspension is excluded, and the Ravens have their own history.

Sources: nflverse game results, ESPN standings, and historical title records. Records are checked daily and advance after a season finishes.

Regular-season records only. Wins carry across seasons, byes, and franchise moves; losses and ties reset the current winning streak. Preseason and postseason games are excluded, with the historical standings exception noted below. Select any threshold from 1+ to 23+. The upper limit is the Colts’ NFL record of 23 consecutive regular-season wins in 2008–09. Every further win in a qualifying run resets its game drought to 0. Green rows have an active streak at or above the selected threshold; yellow rows have exactly one fewer win and are one victory away. Pink rows have never had a qualifying streak; current green/yellow status takes priority.

“Last N+ win” is the most recent victory that left the team on a streak of at least N wins, not the first win of the run or the following loss. Games since counts only that franchise’s completed regular-season games after that victory. Time since is calendar time through the latest game date in the feed; it still passes during offseasons and inactive years. Calendar durations show whole years and months, or days for less than a month. “Best” is the franchise’s longest regular-season winning streak in its covered history, including any current run and runs spanning seasons. The years below it are the season year or range of each record-length run; tied records are included. Season years are used even for games played in January. It is the same record in every threshold view.

Regular-season records only. Losses carry across seasons, byes, and franchise moves; wins and ties reset the losing streak. Preseason and postseason games are excluded, with the historical standings exception noted below. Select any threshold from 1+ to 29+. Green means an active losing streak at or above the selected threshold, not a favorable result. Yellow means one more loss would reach the threshold and end this drought. Pink means the team has never had a qualifying losing streak.

“Last N+ loss” is the most recent defeat that left the team on a streak of at least N losses. Every further loss in a qualifying run resets its game drought to 0. Games since counts completed regular-season games after that defeat; time since is calendar time through the latest game date in the feed. “Worst” means the franchise’s longest losing streak, with the season year or range of every tied record-length run beneath it. Season years are used even for January games. Current losing streaks are included.

All-time top 50 shows up to 50 separate droughts, or every available entry when fewer exist. Choose All teams for the league-wide leaders, or a team for that franchise's own 50 longest droughts, including entries outside the league-wide top 50. The team choice is saved in the URL. It ranks droughts by regular-season games without a qualifying result; calendar days break game-count ties. A franchise can appear more than once. The start is its last qualifying win/loss, or its first league game if it had never qualified. The end is the game that next reached the selected streak length; that drought-ending game is excluded from the game count. Every further win/loss in an already qualifying streak resets the drought, just as in Current. Streaks carry across seasons; ties and the opposite result reset streaks. Preseason and postseason games are excluded. Initial droughts from franchise debut and ongoing droughts are included; ongoing time runs through the latest league game date. Offseasons, byes and suspensions add calendar time but not games. Yellow marks an ongoing drought; pink marks an ongoing drought that has never ended. Coverage follows today's 32 franchises and their predecessors, not separately defunct franchises. Column sorting rearranges the same top 50, not a new top 50 by that column.

Coverage reaches all 32 franchises’ first APFA/NFL, AAFC or 1960 AFL seasons, starting in 1920. Earlier club founding dates and pre-league seasons are not used. The archive is checked against each franchise’s fixed early-season schedule totals and later expected season lengths, including strike-shortened seasons and inactive years. “Never” is used only when covered results extend back to the first league season. In Current's longest-drought sorts, these teams come first, ordered by first league season (oldest first). Their game and time droughts run from their first recorded league-season game. An incomplete history would remain “Not found in covered history” and sort last. Team-name, current-streak and Best/Worst sorts follow their selected columns normally.

Early APFA season records include some games against non-league opponents; these are retained when classified as regular season in the archive, following historical season records. The 1932 Bears–Spartans tiebreaker counted in the regular standings and is included. The 1943 Steagles games are credited to both the Eagles and Steelers, and 1944 Card-Pitt games to both the Cardinals and Steelers. Their opponents see one game against the combined club, not two. The Rams’ 1943 suspension and Browns’ 1996–98 suspension add no games and do not reset streaks.

The current season is included. All win and loss thresholds are checked daily. Defunct and non-league clubs remain separate opponents, and the Ravens have their own history.
