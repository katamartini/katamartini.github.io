NFL droughts: last season and completed seasons without each milestone

Seasons use the NFL season year: a Super Bowl played in February 2026 belongs to the 2025 season. The ongoing season is not counted until its Super Bowl is complete. A result in the latest completed season has a drought of 0.

“Never” counts active seasons in the Super Bowl era, starting in 1966 or the franchise’s first season. Conference titles mean a Super Bowl berth, including the NFL/AFL champions in 1966–69. Earlier league championships are not Super Bowl wins. Division titles include former divisions. Franchise moves and name changes stay with the same team; the Browns’ 1996–98 suspension is excluded, and the Ravens have their own history.

Sources: nflverse game results, ESPN standings, and historical title records. Records are checked weekly and advance after a season finishes.

Regular season only. Wins carry across seasons, byes, and franchise moves; losses and ties reset the current winning streak. Playoff and preseason games are excluded. A run qualifies at five wins. Every further win in a longer run also qualifies, so a current streak of five or more has a game drought of 0.

“Last 5+ win” is the most recent victory that left the team on a streak of at least five wins, not the first win of the run or the following loss. Games since counts only that franchise’s completed regular-season games after that victory. Time since is calendar time through the latest game date in the feed; it still passes during offseasons and inactive years. Calendar durations show whole years and months, or days for less than a month.

The current season is included. Results are checked weekly by the existing Tuesday drought updater. Sources: nflverse game results from 1999 onward, plus the Browns’ 1994 and 1995 schedules to cover their earlier most recent five-win run. Cleveland played no games in 1996–98. The Oilers’ history belongs to the Titans, not the Texans.
